Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life
  • Hot water far hotter than normal, a sign of a runaway thermostat or gas control
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs

The Benefits of Professional Emergency Water Heater Repair

Emergency calls combine electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can leak exhaust into the room if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be fixed at the source, not muted. Capping that valve, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are mistakes we see far too often.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution instead of relying on a sniff test, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a few hundred dollars versus a new heater. You get that answer before any work begins.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
