Signs You Need Water Heater Replacement in Sumter

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Start with the venting. A gas water heater's exhaust contains carbon monoxide, and a vent that is undersized, poorly pitched, or loosely connected can let it spill back into your home. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and there is a lot to get right. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code, and some jurisdictions require a permit. A move to tankless or a heat pump brings gas line sizing, new vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines into the job.

Professional replacement gets all of it right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your household, verify the gas line, vent, and electrical service, and install whatever code items apply. We leak-test, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature before we leave. It is what separates a heater that runs quietly for years from one that has problems from the start. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
