How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve
  • A commercial heater that has grown noticeably louder
  • Rising gas or electric bills with no change in business volume
  • A recirculation loop that no longer seems to do its job
  • An aging unit you would rather replace on your schedule than in an emergency
  • Rust-colored water, leaks at fittings, or moisture under the heater
  • Trouble reaching sanitation temperatures in the kitchen
  • Lukewarm water at the exact time you need the most

Why Professional Commercial Water Heaters Matters

The engineering behind commercial hot water is more demanding. Large gas inputs require confirming the supply pressure, meter size, combustion air, and vent capacity. Commercial electric units may need dedicated high-amperage circuits. High storage temperatures, commonly 140 degrees or above for sanitation, depend on thermostatic mixing valves to bring delivery down to safe levels at hand sinks and showers. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Flush high-demand heaters on a regular interval to keep recovery strong
  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
  • Test thermostatic mixing valves periodically to confirm safe delivery temperatures
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Do not store supplies against the equipment or block air openings
