Many of the installation calls we get in Sumter, SC are not replacements at all. A household outgrows its tank and needs more capacity. A homeowner wants the heater out of a hall closet and into the garage or basement. A remodel needs a second heater on the far side of the house, or a move to all-electric swaps a gas unit for a heat pump model. What these jobs share is new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Sumter Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Sizing and placement decide most of the outcome. For a tank, the first-hour rating is the number we compare against your household's busiest hour; for a tankless unit, we size by flow rate and winter temperature rise. Then comes placement. Gas heaters need a proper vent route and combustion air, heat pump heaters need open space and a condensate drain, and whole-house electric tankless units often need a large dedicated circuit and the panel capacity to match. Put a heater above a finished ceiling and it needs a drain pan; put a gas unit in a garage and code may require a raised platform. We explain the trade-offs plainly and without pressure.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a fast one look nothing alike. Our technicians leak-test every gas joint, set vent pitch and clearances to the manufacturer's spec, match the circuit and breaker to the heater, and use dielectric unions or fittings where copper meets steel. The relief valve discharge line runs full size to where it belongs, and expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are installed when code requires them, with the permit handled when needed. Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • Interest in tankless or heat pump efficiency
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Consider the gas side first. An undersized line starves a new heater whenever the furnace or range runs, and a whole-house tankless unit usually needs more gas than the existing line carries. The vent has to be the right material, size, and pitch, with proper clearances, so exhaust leaves the home instead of drifting back into it. Electric models bring their own math, since breaker, wire gauge, and panel capacity all have to match. Add relief valve piping, dielectric fittings, expansion tanks, drain pans, seismic straps, and permit rules that vary from one jurisdiction to the next, and there are a lot of details to get right.

Professional service handles every trade involved, not just setting the heater. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
