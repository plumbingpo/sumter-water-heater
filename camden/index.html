How Camden Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Long before a tank floods a Camden garage, it almost always sends signals. Look out for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, brownish hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so small problems stay small.
